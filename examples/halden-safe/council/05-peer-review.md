# Stage 2 — anonymized peer review

Four outputs collected, seat names stripped, relabelled A–D in a shuffled order.
One turn. Judged on accuracy and insight only. Mapping disclosed below the line.

## Response A — missing terms

**Accuracy: high. Insight: high.**
Every claim is checkable against the packet, and the central observation is one I
did not make: §2.1 grants a right over "any subsequent issuance of Capital Stock"
with no carve-out for plan issuances, so option grants become notice events. That
is the kind of defect that survives signature and bites for years. The proposed
four-sentence fix to §2 is concrete and could be sent to opposing counsel
tomorrow. Weakness: it ranks pro rata first on a theory of administrative cost
rather than economic exposure, which overstates clause 1 relative to clause 2.

## Response B — risk

**Accuracy: medium. Insight: high.**
Right about the thing that matters most: §4.1 is the only clause in the bundle
that can move the price, and a bridge note with a lower cap transfers real money
with no election and no notice. The trade proposal is the only paragraph in the
four outputs that resembles a plan a founder could act on before Monday.
Weakness: thin on text. It asserts economics without quoting the mechanics, and it
under-ranks §5.2 — calling the conflict clause "overrated" is defensible on money
but wrong on sequencing, because the settlement it proposes assumes §5.2 is gone.
Proposing a trade before securing the clause that governs trades is the one place
it would cost the client.

## Response C — ambiguity

**Accuracy: medium. Insight: medium.**
The list of ambiguous terms is accurate and complete: circular share, undefined
"additional rights", a survival clause with nothing to survive. But the operative
conclusion — that the definition is void and the right fails with it — is a remedy
a court would reach only after refusing to construe, and the drafter here was the
Investor's counsel, so the clause reads against her, not in her favour. A memo
written on this conclusion would tell the founder she has no pro rata obligation,
which is the most expensive possible error in the bundle. Credit for the closing
caveat; the ranking still leads a reader to the wrong place.

## Response D — consistency

**Accuracy: high. Insight: high.**
Best single observation in the set: §1.2 ("amends and complements") and §5.1
("together the entire agreement") cannot both be true, and the drafter has to pick
one. The layered table of MFN scope across five documents is the cleanest artifact
produced by any seat and belongs in the memo largely as written.
Weakness: it concludes that §6.2 extinguishes the side letter, which would make
every other finding moot. That conclusion is arguable but it is the weakest link in
an otherwise strong output, and it is wrong in this instance: §6.2 addresses prior
agreements, and the letter is later in time. If that point is lost, the rest of the
output is unaffected; if it is right, nothing else matters. Put like that, it
should have been argued harder or dropped.
