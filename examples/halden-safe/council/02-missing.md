# Seat B — Missing terms

Prompt: `prompts/reviewer_missing.md`. Ran 18 Nov 2026, one turn, packet only.

## Ranking by exposure to the Company

1. Pro rata §2.1 — no carve-outs, so it captures the option pool
2. MFN §4.1 — no notice, no election, no cap
3. Information rights §3.1 — no sunset, no confidentiality, no use restriction
4. Conflict clause §5.2 — no reciprocal limit
5. Cap math — nothing missing that matters at this size

I rank pro rata first because it is the one place where a missing carve-out turns
an ordinary right into a permanent administrative trap.

## 1. Pro rata — the right has no exclusions

§2.1: the Investor may purchase its Pro Rata Share of "**any subsequent issuance
of Capital Stock** by the Company, until the closing of an Equity Financing" —
[[raw/side-letter.md#2-pro-rata-rights]].

Read literally that includes:

- every option grant and every share issued on exercise under the plan
- the 2,000,000-share pool already reserved and unissued —
  [[raw/cap-table.md#option-pool]]
- shares issued on conversion of the Safe itself
- any future acquisition or advisor grant

No standard pro rata clause works that way. They carve out plan issuances,
conversions, and acquisitions, and they set a price (usually the price paid by the
new investors, sometimes with a discount). This one sets neither price nor
carve-out. Every hiring decision that grants options becomes a notice event, and
every notice event is a chance to argue about the denominator.

- **Also missing:** any statement of the price and terms on which the Investor
  buys. "The right to purchase" with no price is an agreement to agree —
  [[raw/side-letter.md#2-pro-rata-rights]].
- **Also missing:** a minimum or pro-rata-of-pro-rata mechanic If Meridian does not
  take its full share, who gets the balance?

## 2. MFN — no trigger procedure

§4.1 gives an automatic right and §4.2 defines "more favorable" widely —
[[raw/side-letter.md#4-most-favored-nation]]. Missing:

- no duty to notify the Investor that a triggering issuance happened
- no window for the Company to cure or to offer cash instead
- no statement of how the improved terms are papered — a new Safe? an amendment?
- no limit on **how many times** the right can be triggered; it runs to the Equity
  Financing, so every note and every Safe in between resets it

## 3. Information rights — no ending, no confidentiality

§3.1 runs "beginning on the date of this Side Letter" and §6.4 purports to
terminate the letter on an Equity Financing or Liquidity Event, except for
"then-surviving information-rights periods, if any" —
[[raw/side-letter.md#3-information-rights]], [[raw/side-letter.md#6-miscellaneous]].
There are no stated periods, so nothing survives, and the drafting is incoherent
either way.

Missing outright: confidentiality, a use restriction, a restriction on sharing
with the Investor's LPs, and any cap on the on-request covenant —
[[raw/side-letter.md#3-information-rights]].

## 4. Conflict clause — no reciprocity

§5.2 gives the letter precedence without limiting it to the terms the letter
actually addresses — [[raw/side-letter.md#5-conflict-entire-agreement]]. Compare
what a normal clause does: precedence "solely to the extent of the conflict, and
only with respect to the specific provisions addressed in this letter". That is
one sentence and it defuses the whole argument.

Also missing: any clause on how **this letter** is amended. The Safe has one —
[[raw/safe.md#6-miscellaneous]]. The letter does not.

## 5. Cap math

The definitions close every gap I looked for: Company Capitalization is defined,
the pool is inside it, the Safe excludes itself, and the price is the lower of two
stated numbers — [[raw/safe.md#1-definitions]]. Arithmetic in
[[raw/cap-table.md#post-money-math]] checks out.

## What I would change first

Add four sentences to §2: carve-outs (plan, conversions, acquisitions), price
("at the price paid by the new investors"), a 15-day election window, and a
pro-rata-of-pro-rata. Then add one sentence to §5.2 limiting precedence to the
specific provisions addressed.
