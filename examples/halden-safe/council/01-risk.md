## 1. MFN — the only clause that can move the price

Every other clause in this bundle is process. §4.1 can change the terms of a
future round automatically: "the Investor shall automatically receive those more
favorable terms" — [[raw/side-letter.md#4-most-favored-nation]].

Three exposures:

- **The bridge.** §4.1 reaches "any subsequent Safe **or convertible promissory
  note**" — [[raw/side-letter.md#4-most-favored-nation]]. A $250k bridge at a $6M
  cap would automatically give Meridian a $6M cap on its $400k. That is a $100k
  transfer in a single note, and it happens without an election, a notice, or a
  signature.
- **"Additional rights"** in §4.2 is a standing option on every future side
  letter — [[raw/side-letter.md#4-most-favored-nation]]. The Company cannot price
  a later round without knowing what it has already given away.
- **No notice duty.** The Company could be in breach the day it signs a bridge,
  before anyone tells the Investor.

Term sheet scope was "subsequent Safes" only — [[raw/term-sheet.md#investment]].
The widening is 23 days old and is not supported by anything the Company signed.

## 2. Information rights — recurring cost, bounded risk

Monthly statements within 15 days, a budget, an open-ended "such other
information" covenant, and inspection rights — [[raw/side-letter.md#3-information-rights]].

The money cost is real but small: a bookkeeper and four days a month. The
strategic cost is that inspection rights at pre-seed mean an investor in the room
during a fundraise the Company has not announced. Still second, not first: it
cannot change the cap.

## 3. Pro rata — dilution protection the Company can live with

A $400k investor at 4.76% exercising pro rata costs the Company nothing it has not
already budgeted; it prices the next round slightly worse. The draft's mechanics
are bad (see seat D's problem, not mine) but the economics are ordinary —
[[raw/side-letter.md#2-pro-rata-rights]].

## 4. Conflict clause — overrated

§5.2 says the letter controls on conflict — [[raw/side-letter.md#5-conflict-entire-agreement]].
Remove it and the three rights still exist. Its damage is that it makes clauses 1–3
stick harder. It is fourth on money, and first on "cheapest thing to delete".

## 5. Cap math — correct, just misunderstood

$8,000,000 ÷ 10,000,000 = $0.80; $400,000 ÷ $0.80 = 500,000 shares;
500,000 ÷ 10,500,000 = 4.76% — [[raw/cap-table.md#post-money-math]]. The 5.00% is
Purchase Amount ÷ Cap. Nothing to redline. The founder should fix her dilution
model, not the form.
