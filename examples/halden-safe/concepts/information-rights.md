# Information rights

Reporting duties the Company would owe a 4.76% SAFE holder that holds no stock.
The Safe gives none. The draft side letter gives a lot.

## What each document says

- Safe §4: before conversion the Investor has "no information rights, and no
  inspection rights, except as required by law" — [[raw/safe.md#4-no-stockholder-rights]].
- Term sheet: "Major-investor information rights **at Series Seed** (not before)" —
  [[raw/term-sheet.md#investment]].
- Email 03 (founder): reads that as no pre-seed covenant; offers a voluntary
  monthly update email — [[raw/email-03.md#email-03-14-nov-2026]].
- Email 04 (counsel): "'at Series Seed' was the floor, not the ceiling. A lead of
  this size gets monthly financials from day one" — [[raw/email-04.md#email-04-16-nov-2026]].
- Side letter §3.1: monthly unaudited statements within 15 days of month-end, an
  annual board-approved budget, and anything else reasonably requested; §3.2
  inspection rights on reasonable notice — [[raw/side-letter.md#3-information-rights]].

## Three defects in the draft

1. **No end date.** §6.4 terminates the letter on an Equity Financing or a
   Liquidity Event "except that then-surviving information-rights periods, if
   any, continue as written" — [[raw/side-letter.md#6-miscellaneous]]. There are
   no stated periods, so the exception refers to nothing and the termination
   clause arguably saves a right it never bounded.
2. **"Such other information as the Investor may reasonably request"** is an
   open-ended covenant. It is standard in priced rounds with a lead holding 15%+;
   it is not standard for a 4.76% SAFE holder at pre-seed —
   [[raw/side-letter.md#3-information-rights]].
3. **No confidentiality clause anywhere in the letter.** Inspection rights plus
   monthly financials plus no confidentiality undertaking is the worst combination
   in the draft. Confidence medium: absence of a clause is established by reading
   the whole instrument, and a court might imply one.

## Assessment

The founder's instinct is right and her leverage is weak: she argued from a
non-binding term sheet and counsel answered by calling it a floor. The
commercial middle is a monthly update by email, expressly non-contractual, or a
quarterly package with a confidentiality clause and a sunset. What she should not
sign is an unbounded covenant with no end date.
